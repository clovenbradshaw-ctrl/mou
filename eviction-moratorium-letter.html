Emergency Housing Crisis

No One Should Lose Their Home Because of a Disaster

1,350 evictions filed in January 2026 — a 56% increase from last year

In the wake of the winter storm emergency, Nashville families are at risk of losing their homes. We're calling on local leadership to halt evictions and provide emergency rental assistance now.

The Crisis

Why This Matters Right Now

Prior to the state of emergency, hundreds of Nashvillians were already facing eviction. The winter storm has made everything worse—power outages, lost income, and unsafe housing conditions have pushed more families to the brink.

183 Evictions in One Week

Between last Monday and Saturday alone, 183 evictions were filed by 109 landlords—during a declared state of emergency.

February 5th Deadline

Rent due February 1st becomes legally late on February 5th. Families behind on payments will soon face eviction filings.

Research Shows the Pattern

Eviction filings spike after weather emergencies, especially in states like Tennessee with weak tenant protections.

The "Hands Tied" Myth

Nashville Has the Power to Act

Metro officials claim preemption by the State ties their hands. But legal experts have identified a clear path forward through the Detainer Statute—which governs procedure, not substance, and lacks preemption language.

What Nashville Can Do

  • Procedural Eviction Ordinance: A local law based on the Detainer Statute that delays the timeline of eviction filings—creating a mandatory cooling-off period.
  • Sheriff Accountability: The Sheriff's Office can deprioritize "set-outs" (physical removals) during the crisis—an operational choice, not a legal one.
  • Automatic Continuances: Judges can grant automatic continuances for tenants with pending rental assistance applications or those without utilities.
  • Emergency Rental Assistance: Make relief funds available immediately through partners who can distribute them quickly.

Even a law that is eventually challenged provides a "fighting chance." A local ordinance is law until a judge says it isn't. In that time, families can find new jobs, children can finish school semesters, and tenants can secure federal rental assistance.

Our Demands

What We Need From Local Leadership

1. Emergency Rental Assistance

Increase the availability and accessibility of low-barrier, timely emergency rental assistance. Funds from the Winter Storm Recovery Fund must be made available immediately for rent relief through partners who can distribute them quickly.

2. Pause Eviction Dockets

General Sessions Court should pause eviction hearings and grant automatic continuances until the state of emergency and its immediate impacts have ended. Knoxville closed their eviction court both last week and this week.

3. Halt Eviction Executions

The Sheriff's Office should immediately suspend the execution of eviction orders. Emergency shelters are already at capacity—forcibly displacing residents now would increase homelessness and strain an overburdened system.

4. Pass a Procedural Ordinance

Metro Council should use the "procedural lane" in the Detainer Statute to create a mandatory delay on eviction filings. Nashville has Home Rule authority—if the state hasn't explicitly said no, the city can act.
